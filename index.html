    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is the difference between interior design and interior architecture?
    Interior architecture focuses on the structural and spatial aspects of a building's interior—walls, load-bearing elements, and major layout changes. Interior design is more about the finishing layer: furniture, color, lighting, and decor. In practice, the two overlap significantly, and many professionals work across both disciplines.
    How do I choose a color palette for my home?
    Start with the fixed elements you can't easily change—flooring, countertops, and large furniture. Pull 2–3 neutral base tones from those, then add one or two accent colors that you genuinely love. Test paint samples on multiple walls and observe them at different times of day before committing.
    What are the most common interior design mistakes?
    The most frequent mistakes include: ignoring scale (furniture that's too big or too small for the room), insufficient lighting layers, pushing all furniture against walls, and choosing trends over timelessness. Good design is about how a space feels and functions, not just how it photographs.
    Is sustainable design more expensive?
    Not necessarily. While some sustainable materials and technologies have a higher upfront cost, they often save money over time through energy efficiency and durability. Simple choices—like using reclaimed wood, choosing local materials, and designing for passive heating and cooling—can be cost-neutral or even cheaper.
    How can I make a small room feel larger?
    Use light colors on walls and ceilings, choose furniture with legs to show more floor, hang curtains high and wide, use mirrors strategically, and keep clutter to a minimum. Vertical storage and multi-functional pieces also help maximize every square foot.
